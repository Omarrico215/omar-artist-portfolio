About the artist

Rooted in memory.
Made in community.

Omar Buenaventura is a self-taught Filipino-American installation artist based in Philadelphia. His practice draws from childhood in the Philippines, life on Lenapehoking, and the traditions carried through family and community.

Working with reclaimed wood, locally grown bamboo, rusted metal, plants, and natural dyes, Omar gives discarded materials another life. His rustic, asymmetrical work embraces imperfections as traces of history and character.

“Honoring the past through art and practice, I believe my work is an act of resistance.”

The Dazzling Magic of Filipino Lanterns

HYPERALLERGIC · DECEMBER 27, 2023

Hyperallergic features Omar Buenaventura and his work keeping the Filipino tradition of parol-making alive in Philadelphia. The article highlights the annual lanterns he creates with his family and a community workshop with Bahay215 co-founder Nicky Uy, using bamboo, string, colorful paper, and rice or cornstarch paste.

Omar describes his vision as bringing a hundred stars to Philadelphia—sharing the light and joy of this Filipino tradition with the community.

Collective practice

Bahay215

Founded in 2020 by Omar Buenaventura and Nicky Uy, Bahay215 is a Philadelphia-based collaboration exploring Filipinx culture, diaspora, plants, natural materials, and community care. The work makes space for shared knowledge and everyday acts of cultural connection.

Exhibition · Schuylkill Center for Environmental Education

Companions – mas masarap magkasama

A collaborative exhibition with Filipino-American artist Maria Dumlao and Bahay215 (Omar Buenaventura and Nicky Uy), exploring how food, plants, ecology, and cultural memory shape our sense of home and belonging. The project brought together interactive prints and site-specific installations, including bamboo structures inspired by the Filipino bahay kubo. “Mas masarap magkasama” means “more delicious together.”

Featured collaboration

Heirloom Plants: Ancestral Seeds in Philadelphia

Bahay215 is featured as a community partner in this exhibition at the Academy of Natural Sciences, celebrating Philadelphia gardens where ancestral plants, cultural heritage, and community science meet. The project documents heirloom plants alongside the stories, recipes, and traditional knowledge shared by seed keepers and gardeners—honoring how communities carry seeds and foodways across generations and make a home in Philadelphia.
